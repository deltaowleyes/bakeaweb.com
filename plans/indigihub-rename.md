# Plan: Renaming the IndigiHub version

Task id: `indigihub-rename` · Written 2026-11-08 · Planning only (no code)

**Why:** an award-winning Indigenous-led platform called IndigiHub already runs at indigihub.com (see `plans/names-and-trademarks-check.md`). This plan is the **fallback** if Zachary Flett and IndigiHub would rather not partner, or don't reply within about 3 weeks. If they do partner, we may keep the name with their written OK and this plan isn't needed.

> Not legal advice. Every name below still needs a trademark check (5 minutes on CIPO, steps at the end) and, ideally, a lawyer's quick look.

## The short version

1. **Don't pick the name ourselves.** Gather a small group of Indigenous advisors (3 to 5 people, paid for their time) and let them choose or reshape the name. The lists below are only a starting point for them to react to.
2. **The safest starting ideas are plain English** and make no claim to any Nation's language, symbols or ceremonies: *Nations Web*, *Our Nations Web*, *Homeland Web*, *Many Voices Web*.
3. **The cheapest choice** is to put the free Indigenous pages under BAKE's own name (for example *BAKE Nations*), which needs no new domain at all.
4. **Costs are small:** a `.ca` domain is about $8 for the first year and $9 a year after that. The real cost is the advisors' time (roughly $1,000 to $2,500 in total, see §5).
5. **Switching takes about one day of work** once the name is chosen, because nothing has launched publicly yet.

## 1. What a good name needs

- **Clearly different from IndigiHub.** No "Indigi", no "Hub".
- **Not already used by an Indigenous organisation.** For example, *Many Nations* is out: it's the name of a national Indigenous co-operative (Many Nations Financial Services, Saskatoon). *Native Web* is out too: NativeWeb has been an Indigenous internet organisation since the 1990s.
- **No borrowed culture.** A non-Indigenous company shouldn't use words from an Indigenous language, sacred items (eagle feathers, medicine wheels, sweetgrass), ceremonies, or terms like *Turtle Island*, *Fire Keeper* or *Treaty* unless the advisors propose them and the right community agrees. Some of these look harmless but carry real meaning. *Kinship*, for example, is central to Cree teachings (wâhkohtowin) and to Indigenous child-welfare work.
- **Works in English and French**, is easy to spell, and reads well as `name.ca/pagename`.
- **Has a matching domain** at a fair yearly price (watch the renewal price, not just the first year).
- **Passes a CIPO check** (no similar marks in classes 35 and 42).

## 2. Name ideas for the advisors

Domains checked on 2026-11-08 by looking them up on the internet. "Looks free" means no website answered, which **usually** means the domain can be bought. Confirm on Porkbun before relying on it (§4).

### Group A: plain English, no cultural claim (safest starting point)

| Name | Domain to try | Domain looks | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nations Web** | `nationsweb.ca` | free | Short and clear. `nationsweb.com` is taken by someone else. |
| **Our Nations Web** | `ournationsweb.com` / `ournations.site` | free / free | "Our" works best if the advisors say Indigenous owners would say it about themselves. |
| **Homeland Web** | `homelandweb.ca` | free | Warm, but "homeland" can mean very different things to different Nations and to Métis people. Ask. |
| **Many Voices Web** | `manyvoicesweb.ca` / `manyvoices.site` | free / free | Fits languages and syllabics support. Not Indigenous-specific in wording. |
| **Gathering Web** | `gatheringweb.ca` | free | `gatheringweb.com` is taken. "Gathering" is widely used for Indigenous events; ask whether that's welcome. |
| **Circle of Sites** | `circleofsites.com` | free | The circle is a common Indigenous symbol (Many Nations' own logo uses one). Ask first. |

### Group B: under the BAKE brand (cheapest, clearest ownership)

| Name | Address | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **BAKE Nations** | `bakednations.com` (looks free) or simply `bakedby.me/pagename` with a Nations badge | Honest about who runs it: a BAKE service, not an Indigenous-owned organisation. No new domain needed if we use bakedby.me. Some people may dislike pairing "baked" (cannabis slang) with Nations; ask. |
| **BAKE Community** | `communitybake.com` (looks free) or on bakedby.me | Broadest, but loses the Indigenous focus. |

### Group C: only if the advisors propose them

A name in Cree, Ojibwe, Inuktitut, Michif or another language, or one built on cultural imagery, can be the most meaningful choice, but only if it comes **from** the advisors and the speakers of that language, with their permission for us to use it on a business service. If they suggest one, also ask:
- Whose language is it, and who should give permission?
- Should the name be owned (or the trademark held) by an Indigenous partner rather than by us?
- Are there syllabics or special characters? Domains can hold them, but they're harder to type, so we'd also want a plain-letter domain.

### Taken or ruled out

`manynations.ca` (Many Nations co-op), `circleweb.ca`, `firstweb.ca`, `homefire.site`, `homelands.site`, `firekeeper.site`, `kinshipweb.com`, `nationsweb.com`, `gatheringweb.com`: already in use. *Treaty Web*: treaties are legal and political agreements, too heavy for a website brand.

## 3. Domain prices at Porkbun (US dollars, checked online November 2026)

| Ending | First year | Each year after | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `.ca` | about $8 | about $9 | **Best value and clearly Canadian.** You must meet CIRA's Canadian presence rules (a Canadian resident or business is fine). |
| `.com` | about $11 | about $11 | Expected to rise about 7% a year until 2030. |
| `.site` | about $2 | about **$29** | Cheap to start, expensive to keep. |
| `.cloud` (what indigihub.cloud uses) | about $3 | about **$21** | Same trap. |

**Recommendation:** use a `.ca`, plus the `.com` if it's free, to stop someone else grabbing it. About $20 for the first year, about $20 a year after.

**What to do with indigihub.cloud:** keep it for one more year (about $21) and forward it to the new address, so nobody else grabs it and causes confusion. If IndigiHub would like it, offer to transfer it to them for free, which is a nice goodwill gesture. Otherwise let it lapse after a year.

## 4. How to check a name yourself

1. **Domain:** go to https://porkbun.com/, type the name (for example `nationsweb.ca`) in the search box and press Enter. Note the price **and** the renewal price. Don't buy yet.
2. **Trademark:** go to https://ised-isde.canada.ca/cipo/trademark-search/srch, type the name, press Search, and look for results in classes 35 or 42 (see the names plan for details).
3. **Web:** search the name plus "Indigenous" on Google to spot organisations already using it.
4. Send me what you find, or press the BAKE button below and I'll write up the comparison.

## 5. Advisory circle: how it could work (suggestion for you to shape)

- **Who:** 3 to 5 Indigenous people, ideally a mix of First Nations, Inuit and Métis, including at least one small-business owner and one language speaker. Ways to find them: the Indigenous Chamber of Commerce Manitoba, CCIB (Canadian Council for Indigenous Business), local Friendship Centres, or IndigiHub itself if the partnership talk goes well.
- **Paid, not volunteer:** offer an honorarium per meeting. Common amounts in Canada run about $100 to $300 per person per session, more for Elders or Knowledge Keepers (ask what's customary for each person). **Two sessions × 4 people × $150 to $300 ≈ $1,200 to $2,400.**
- **Session 1 (1 hour, online):** show the studio and the plan, share the lists above, ask what feels right and wrong, and invite their own ideas.
- **Session 2 (1 hour):** check the shortlist (2 or 3 names), the logo direction, and the review rules from `VISION.md` §3. Pick the name.
- **Afterwards:** credit the circle on the about page (if they want), and ask whether they'd stay on as the ongoing advisory circle that `VISION.md` already calls for.
